How it works

1. What each stat is worth. Fit on {{ "{:,}".format(pr.weights.games) }} D-I team-games from last season: each team's opponent-adjusted margin per 100 possessions, regressed on its box score per 100 possessions (the stats explain {{ (pr.weights.r2_net * 100)|round|int }}% of the game-to-game margin). Weights are set so a turnover costs one average possession, so they read as points above an average use of the ball.

2. Per player. The weights are applied to each player's stats per 100 possessions on the floor. Each assist takes {{ (pr.weights.assist_share * 100)|round|int }}% of an average basket's value from the scorer and gives it to the passer.

3. Small samples. A player's own numbers count half after 250 possessions on the floor (about 9 full games). Until then he leans on last season's rating (any D-I player, transfers included, regressed 30%) or a newcomer baseline of −1.

4. Tied to the team. Every player on a team moves by the same amount so the five-man total matches the team's adjusted rating. A team that wins more than its box score says shares that credit.

Known lean. Like any box-score rating, it rewards efficient bigs (rebounds, blocks, high-percentage shots) and is hard on high-usage, lower-efficiency creators, whose value partly shows up in teammates' open shots. The on/off layer in December is the check on that.
