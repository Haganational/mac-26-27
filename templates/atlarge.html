How it works

The ranking is meant to be read at a glance and checked by hand. A team's watch rank is its strength rank, moved up by its league's recent at-large record.

…
  • Strength. Our ratings solve offense, defense and pace for every D-I team from every box score, adjusted for opponent and venue (the same model as each edition's ratings page). The NCAA's NET joins in once it is published; we average the two ranks.
  • League boost. A conference that has averaged one at-large bid a year lifts its teams {{ boost|int }} spots. Strong leagues give their teams more chances at the Quadrant 1 wins the committee looks for, and the boost is a simple stand-in for that.
  • A bigger field. The 2027 tournament grows from 68 teams to 76, so there are about 44 at-large spots instead of 36. The zone lines sit a little lower than they would have last year for that reason.
  • Recency. A tournament's weight halves every {{ half_life|int }} years, so the last few Marches count most.
  • Know the limits. The Mountain West's at-large record was built largely by San Diego State, Utah State, Boise State, Colorado State and Fresno State, who left for the Pac-12 in July 2026. The boost goes by the league's own history, so the Mountain West's may run high this season. Early-season ratings also move a lot week to week.
